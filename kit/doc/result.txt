The throughput approximately doubles each time the buffer 
size is doubled at smaller buffer sizes. As the buffer size 
becomes larger, the increase in throughput gradually becomes
smaller, eventually leveling off at about 16000 MB/sec.

Larger buffers reduce the number of read and write system
calls needed to process the given file. Thus, the performance
initially increases significantly. However, once the buffer
becomes large, reducing the number of such system calls further
makes less difference because most of the remaining time is 
spent actually moving the data. Therefore increasing the buffer
size has diminishing returns, and that is why it appears to 
approach an asymptotic limit.
